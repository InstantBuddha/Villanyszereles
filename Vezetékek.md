### What H07RN-F actually is

**H07RN-F 5G4** is a **heavy-duty flexible rubber cable**, typically used for:

- Temporary outdoor power
    
- Construction sites
    
- Generators
    
- Extension leads
    
- Industrial equipment
    

Breakdown:

- **H** – Harmonised (EU standard)
    
- **07** – Rated **450/750 V**
    
- **RN** – Rubber insulation + neoprene/PCP outer sheath
    
- **F** – Flexible
    
- **5G4** – 5 cores, including earth, 4 mm² each
    

It’s _very_ rugged — oil-resistant, UV-resistant, weather-resistant.

But rugged ≠ burial-rated.

---

## Why permanent burial is not allowed 🚫

### 1. **Chemical attack from soil**

Soil isn’t neutral:

- Acids, alkalis, fertilizers
    
- Organic decay products
    
- Groundwater contaminants
    

The rubber compounds in **H07RN-F slowly degrade in soil**, especially when permanently wet. Burial-rated cables use **special PE or XLPE jackets** that are chemically stable underground for decades.

---

### 2. **Moisture ingress over time**

H07RN-F:

- Is water-resistant
    
- Is **not water-blocked**
    

In soil:

- Water sits against the jacket permanently
    
- Capillary action can pull moisture along the conductors
    
- Freeze/thaw cycles pump water deeper
    

This eventually leads to:

- Insulation resistance failure
    
- Corrosion of conductors
    
- Tripping RCDs or worse
    

---

### 3. **Mechanical damage risk**

Underground cables face:

- Stones and sharp edges
    
- Ground movement and settling
    
- Root growth
    
- Accidental digging
    

Flexible rubber cables **compress and deform**, unlike solid-jacket underground cables designed to withstand constant pressure.

---

### 4. **Thermal issues**

Buried cables:

- Cannot dissipate heat well
    
- Must be designed for soil thermal resistivity
    

H07RN-F is rated assuming **air cooling or free movement**, not constant soil contact. This affects current-carrying capacity and long-term insulation life.

---

### 5. **Standards & liability**

This is the big one.

Electrical codes (IEC, VDE, BS 7671, etc.) **explicitly require cables with a burial rating** for underground installation.

If you bury H07RN-F:

- It violates installation standards
    
- Insurance can be void
    
- Liability shifts entirely to the installer
    
- Inspectors will fail it immediately
    

Even if it “works fine” for years.

---
#### What _should_ be used instead ✅

Depending on region:

- **NYY-J**
    
- **N2XY / N2XH**
    
- **AXMK / AXQJ**
    
- **UF-rated cable** (North America)
    

These are:

- Moisture-resistant
    
- Chemically stable
    
- Mechanically robust
    
- Explicitly certified for direct burial
    

If flexibility is needed → conduit + proper underground cable.
